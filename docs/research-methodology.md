# Research methodology

## What informed the work

The project brief frames the work as informed by accessibility principles and
guidance, research-informed design hypotheses, founder-led product exploration,
and iterative implementation and testing. It does not identify specific
guidance documents, research sources, study activities, or test records.
Consequently, this page does not claim a completed literature review,
participant study, or independently verified implementation cycle.

These evidence types have different limits:

- **Accessibility principles and guidance** can inform design questions and
  requirements, but do not by themselves establish that a product works for
  people.
- **Design hypotheses** make assumptions explicit and suggest behaviors to
  evaluate; they are not user findings.
- **Founder-led exploration and checks** can identify product questions and
  functional issues, but are not a substitute for disabled people's
  perspectives.
- **Implementation evidence** can show what code was written, but does not
  prove that behavior works throughout the product or meets access needs.

## Proposed future participatory research framework

The following is a proposal, not completed participant research.

1. **Plan with disabled people.** Involve people with relevant lived
   experience in shaping questions, session materials, and interpretation.
2. **Compensate participation.** Agree fair compensation in advance and explain
   payment and scheduling clearly.
3. **Obtain informed consent.** Explain the purpose, activities, recording,
   data handling, and withdrawal options in accessible language. Seek
   permission separately for any recording or quotation.
4. **Offer accessible formats.** Provide options for remote or in-person
   sessions, breaks, advance materials, and accessible documents or
   communication tools as appropriate.
5. **Support communication preferences.** Ask participants how they prefer to
   communicate and allow alternatives to spoken responses.
6. **Keep disclosure under participant control.** Do not require a diagnosis
   or ask for unnecessary personal or medical details.
7. **Handle feedback safely.** Collect only necessary information, restrict
   access, remove identifying details from shared notes, and agree how
   recordings and notes will be retained or deleted.
8. **Translate findings carefully.** With permission, turn actionable findings
   into clearly scoped GitHub issues that describe the observation, evidence,
   proposed next step, and validation needed. Do not include identifying
   participant information.
9. **Close the loop.** Communicate back to participants how their input
   influenced decisions, what changed, and what could not be addressed, in an
   agreed accessible format.

Research design, consent, compensation, data handling, and participant safety
should be reviewed with appropriate expertise before recruitment begins.
